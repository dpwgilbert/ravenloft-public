## A Path Chosen

The damp ash of Barovia still clung to the hem of "Fabulous" Rick Landry’s velvet cloak, but inside the tavern, he sat buffing a silver coin against his sleeve. Around the table, his companions bore the physical and mental scars of the Death House.

[[Claire Ich Al-ErRoar|Claire]] was softly chanting prayers over her bruises, her cleric’s symbol glowing with tired divine light. [[Kexik]] sat rigid, polishing a paladin’s armor that had seen far too much cultist blood, while [[Specimen - 88|88]] slammed a heavy tankard onto the wood, the barbarian’s breath still ragged from sheer anger. Across the table, [[Zavran Moonshadow|Zavran]] flipped through a scorched spellbook, trying to transcribe whatever dark arcana they’d scraped off the walls, and [[Jerry Rig|Jerry]] was furiously disassembling a mechanical cog that had jammed during their desperate escape.

Rick looked at them all—a paladin fighting for honor, a cleric for faith, a wizard for secrets, an artificer for function, and a titan for rage. They had survived horrors that turned men’s hair white, and what had it cost them? Ripped cloaks, spent spell slots, chipped blades, and near-fatal trauma.

_Honor doesn't pay for silk repair,_ Rick thought, flipping the silver coin high into the air and catching it with a soft snap. _And faith doesn't put a price tag on a escape route._\
In the dark corridors of that cursed mansion, while [[Specimen - 88|88]] broke down doors and [[Kexik]] held the line, Rick had noticed something the others missed. He had seen the pristine marble hearths amidst the rot, the exquisite vintage of poisoned wine, the desperate leverage hidden in forgotten deeds, and the raw, transactional nature of survival. Horror operated on debt and sacrifice—a grotesque economy of soul and flesh.

If the multiverse was going to try to kill "Fabulous" Rick Landry, it was going to have to negotiate first.

He leaned back, resting his boots on an empty chair, a slow, dazzling smirk returning to his face. Songs of grand heroics were fine for romantic fools, but true power? True power lay in supply chains, contracts, valuation, and knowing precisely what a man—or a dark lord—would pay to get what they wanted. He wouldn't just be a bard strumming tunes for copper; he would master the School of Mercantile. He would trade in favors, leverage, fine goods, and ironclad bargains.

"Jerry," Rick said, sliding the silver coin across the table toward the artificer. "Find out what metal that ghost’s pocket watch was made of. If we’re going back into the dark, darling, we’re at least making a profit."
